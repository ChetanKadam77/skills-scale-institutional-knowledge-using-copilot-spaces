# OctoAcme Project Management Documentation

## Overview

OctoAcme uses a structured, customer-centered project management approach designed to deliver value through clear ownership, iterative execution, and informed decision-making. The methodology emphasizes collaboration across product, delivery, QA, and stakeholder groups, while keeping work aligned to customer outcomes and measurable results.

The project management process is organized around a lifecycle that moves from initiation to planning, execution, release, and retrospective improvement. This ensures projects are not only delivered efficiently, but also continuously improved based on evidence, feedback, and lessons learned.

## Key Principles

- Customer-first: prioritize customer value, usability, and business impact
- Iterative delivery: ship small, testable increments to reduce risk
- Clear ownership: assign accountability for outcomes, milestones, and communication
- Data-informed decisions: use metrics, feedback, and evidence to guide adjustments
- Psychological safety: encourage open feedback, learning, and continuous improvement

## Core Roles

- Project Manager (PM): coordinates delivery, schedules, risks, and stakeholder communication
- Product Manager (PdM): defines outcomes, prioritizes work, and measures success
- Developers: design and implement solutions with a focus on quality and testability
- QA/Testing: validates quality, acceptance criteria, and release readiness
- Stakeholders: provide input, approvals, and business context

## Lifecycle Stages

1. Initiation
   - Validate the problem, stakeholders, and business need
   - Align on scope, outcomes, and high-level timeline

2. Planning
   - Define project scope, milestones, dependencies, and resource needs
   - Build a practical roadmap for execution

3. Execution and Tracking
   - Deliver work in iterative cycles
   - Monitor progress, risks, dependencies, and quality

4. Release and Deployment
   - Prepare for production release
   - Validate readiness, communicate changes, and monitor impact

5. Retrospective and Continuous Improvement
   - Capture lessons learned
   - Identify improvements for future cycles
